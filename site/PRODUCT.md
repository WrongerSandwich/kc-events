# Product

## Register

product

## Platform

web

## Users

Anyone in the Kansas City metro, on either side of the state line, planning what to do this weekend or in the next few weeks. Often on a phone, often deciding with friends, often arriving from a link someone shared (a /r/kansascity post, a coworker, a group chat). Evan and his friends are this person too, not a separate audience. The job: get a clear picture of what's worth doing without wading through ads or a feed, then pick something and go.

## Product Purpose

A weekly researched, ad-free list of what's worth doing in Kansas City. A research job reads venue and organizer pages directly each week; the site shows the result as an editorial front page (the don't-miss list, grouped by horizon, with always-there events below) and an explorer that slices every active event by date, kind, region, and search, with every slice shareable as a URL. Events can be saved in the browser and exported to a calendar.

Success is a stranger from /r/kansascity coming back, and Evan proposing something to friends that he found here and would not have found otherwise.

## Positioning

The only KC events list that tells you which things you'd regret missing, with a reason for each, and that publishes a date only when it was read from the event's own page.

## Brand Personality

Friendly, warm, easygoing. It reads like a well-informed friend who has already done the looking: relaxed, never pushy, never selling. Warmth comes from the voice and the care in the layout, not from hype. Plain language, nouns for headings ("This weekend", "Always there"), no exclamation marks, no slogans. Honest about what it knows: last-verified dates and the staleness banner are part of the tone, not fine print.

## Anti-references

- Ad-laden local aggregators: thumbnails, sponsored slots, undifferentiated volume. The site exists because these are unreadable.
- Ticketing marketplaces (Eventbrite, Ticketmaster): big event images, countdowns, scarcity pressure, buy buttons.
- Social feeds: infinite scroll, engagement bait, algorithmic ordering.

## Design Principles

1. **Calm density.** Many events readable at a glance is the thing the aggregators fail at. Space separates; rules are thin and few; nothing competes for attention.
2. **Trust is visible.** Every date can be traced to its source, and the page says when it was last checked. Never imply more certainty than the data has.
3. **The page is the picture.** No imagery; type, color, and order carry the identity. A page of text should look cared for.
4. **A friend's pick, not a pitch.** Don't-miss calls come with a short reason and no urgency. The reader brings the taste; the site brings the structure.
5. **Respect the visitor.** No ads, accounts, tracking, or third-party requests; fast on a phone; state stays in the visitor's browser.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

WCAG 2.2 AA: every text and chip pairing clears 4.5:1 in both light and dark schemes. Semantic landmarks, one h1 per page, keyboard-operable filters with visible focus, chips as real buttons with `aria-pressed`, a live region for filter results. Motion is minimal and honors `prefers-reduced-motion`. Kind is never conveyed by color alone.
