# An event's identity is its primary page plus its normalized title

Deduplication could key on (title, date, venue), but a date can change and still be the same event, and keying on primary URL alone breaks when a venue uses one calendar URL for every show that month. So an incoming event matches an existing record when its primary URL and normalized title match; failing that, a fuzzy match on normalized title, venue, and a date within a few days. Ids are assigned at first-seen and never change, so a date move is an update that triggers re-judging rather than a new record.

**Amended (#16):** the fuzzy match treats two venue names as one venue when they normalize alike, when one contains the other, or when a hand-kept venue alias list names both. A sighting with no venue matches on title and the same calendar date alone, but only when exactly one known event qualifies. Two records that come to match each other are folded: the one seen first is kept, and the other expires as `duplicate`.

**Amended (#32):** on the same calendar date, in the fuzzy and venue-less matches, a title also matches itself (or itself cut short) with a series name in front, set off by a bar, a colon, a dash, or a spaced hyphen ("Pershing Lecture Series | A" is "A"). The shorter title must start right after a separator in the longer, so two events in one series stay two. Same date only, because twins of this kind are two readings of one listing, and a nearby date would fold "Open Mic" into Thursday's "Comedy Night: Open Mic". The exact rule (same page and title, any date) is not widened: it ignores the date, and same-page twins that name a venue already match fuzzily. A title worded differently ("w/" for "with", a trailing tag in braces) is still a different title: matching on similar wording would merge distinct events at a venue that runs many similarly named ones.

**Amended (#28):** two venue names made of the same words in another order are one venue ("New American Royal Campus" is "American Royal New Campus"), with no alias needed. It takes the same words, not just shared ones: "The Hall at Union Station" and "Union Station Hall" stay two venues unless an alias joins them. A name with a single-letter or numbered label in it is left out ("Studio A Room B" is not "Studio B Room A"). Like the other venue rules, it applies to alias entries too.

**Amended (#63):** a dated record folds into a recurring record on the same primary page when its title is the recurring event's with a month tag after it: a month's name or abbreviation in parentheses, with or without a period, optionally with a year ("Main Gallery Tours (Oct. 2026)" is "Main Gallery Tours"). A page may title a standing tour month by month, and one reading of it may still give a month as a dated event. The recurring record is kept even when it was seen later, and keeps its own reading: it is the event, and the dated record only one reading of one month. Only the month tag is set aside; any other difference in title, or another primary page, is a different event. Two recurring records still never fold.

**Amended (#77):** a primary page is the same page whichever way its URL is written: without `www.`, over http or https, with or without a trailing slash, with or without a fragment; a query string still makes another page. Stored URLs keep the form the page was fetched under, and are what the run fetches and the site links; identity, a page's standing, and the run's record of which pages it tried go through the key. Two records on one page with one title are the same event only when their dates are within the fuzzy window: a page that lists a title twice, weeks apart, lists two shows, and within one page's reading a verified candidate with a known event's title on a far date, where the page also gives the event on or near its own date, starts a second record, whichever night the page gives first. A sighting still matches a record on page and title whatever the date, so a lone far date is still a date move; among several such records it takes the one whose date is nearest. A cancellation applies only to a record whose date is within the window of the cancelled listing's. A venue-less sighting with two or more qualifying events prefers the one on its own page, then the one on its host, then the one whose own page is its own; two venue-less records on one host with one title on one date fold as twins; where neither narrows it to one, it is ambiguous, creates nothing, and is counted in the run report, so venue-less twins no longer block each other and no longer multiply. A record folded as a duplicate names the record it was folded into (`duplicateOf`), and a sighting that matches the duplicate is a sighting of that record; a duplicate folded before the pointer was kept matches nothing, as before. The month-tag rule (#63) applies to sightings too: a dated sighting that is one month of a recurring event on its page confirms the recurring event and creates no record, and never refreshes or cancels it. An alias entry is matched by a venue name on equality, word reordering, or the entry with a label added ("Bartle Exhibit Hall (A – E)" for "Bartle Exhibit Hall"), no longer by a name shorter than the entry: "Kansas City" was every venue in two alias groups. A short form in use is an entry of its own ("Kauffman Center").

**Amended (#91):** an alias entry made only of generic words ("Pavilion", "Main Auditorium", "Galleries") is matched by that name alone, not by word reordering or a label added: "Swope Park Pavilion" is another venue's pavilion, and since the site now publishes the venue an alias files a name under, a looser match would print the wrong building. The alias list is global, so an entry is one building's rooms only; separate buildings under one name (a library's branches) would fold same-titled programs at each into one event.

**Amended (#66):** two records with the same own page (`eventUrl`, compared as a page is, see #77) on the same calendar date are one event, whatever their titles and venues, provided their times agree when both give one. A page may bill one show under the act one week and under its tour or "An Evening with" the next ("Legacy Tour 2026" for "Udit Narayan & Aditya Narayan"), and the title rules above cannot join those. A detail or ticket page names one show, so it is the stronger key. Same date only: an exhibition's page and a series page serve several dates. Venue is not compared: a reading may name no venue, or name the presenter's space instead of the host's ("Marble & Canvas" at the Kansas City Art Institute). The rule applies to sightings and to folding alike, so pairs already in the dataset fold (the one seen first is kept), and the title follows the latest reading like any other field; a title that changes is a line in the run report's identity churn. When it was decided (2026-10-12), 11 live pairs on 8 sources matched, and all 11 were the same event.

**Amended (#68):** two records that both give an end date, on the same calendar date, with the same or shortened title and the same venue, are one event, however far apart their starts are or when one gives no start. A limited run is named by when it closes: one source gives an exhibition's opening, another the day it listed it, and a third (a homepage that only says "through December 6") no start at all, and the fuzzy window, which compares starts, held each of those as its own record. The end must be given by both, and is compared as a calendar date; a time on it is not. Title and venue are compared as in the fuzzy match, and a record with no venue never qualifies (the venue-less rule still needs a start date), so the same end at two venues stays two events. Like the fuzzy match, it applies to sightings and to folding alike, and the fold keeps the record seen first. When it was decided (2026-10-12), 12 live pairs matched, five Nerman Museum exhibitions held as 14 records, and all were the same exhibition.

**Amended (#119):** two recurring records at the same venue are one event when their titles are the same, on any page, or when one title is the other cut short at a word boundary, on the same primary page. A recurring record has no date, so the fuzzy and venue-less matches, which need a start, never reached it, and only the exact rule (same page, same title) could. That left a market read from two hosts of its own site ("Farmers Market" on thecitymarket.org and thecitymarketkc.org) and a district's night read under its short and full names on one page ("First Friday", "First Friday in the Crossroads") as two published records each. The venue is compared as in the fuzzy match, aliases included, and a record with no venue never qualifies, so "First Friday @ Apex Artspace" stays a gallery's own night. A shortened title joins only on one page: "Open Mic" and "Open Mic Comedy" at one venue on two pages stay two. The rule applies to sightings and to folding alike, and the fold keeps the record seen first; of the pair's readings, the one with the fuller schedule is what the kept record ends with, so a schedule that only repeats the title ("First Friday") gives way to one that says when ("First Friday of every month"). When it was decided (2026-10-12), two live pairs matched, both the same event; a dated record never matches under this rule, and two recurring records still differ when one is a month of the other (#63's month-tag rule is unchanged).
