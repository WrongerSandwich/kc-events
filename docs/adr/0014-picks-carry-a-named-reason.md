# A pick carries a named reason from a fixed vocabulary; scarcity is one of six

Through 2026-11-08 the front page asked one question of every one-off and limited run: would a friend be sorry to have missed it because it will not come around again (ADR 0005)? The structure did what it was meant to, a stated reason on every pick, no opaque score, stability between runs, and it also produced a list nobody chose. Of the 14 picks on 2026-11-04, eight were music and two were talks; theater, film, comedy, festivals and community events had none between them, and one of 56 limited runs was flagged against 13 of 360 one-offs. Scarcity of occurrence selects for the lone visiting act or figure, which is the most taste-specific thing on any list, while what a mixed group of friends actually gets out the door for (a festival, a once-a-year tradition, a name everyone knows, something strange and new, the production of the season) recurs, runs for weeks, or is not rare at all, and so failed the test. Scarcity is anti-correlated with broad appeal. It is one reason an event belongs on the front page, not the only one.

A pick now carries one reason from a closed vocabulary of six, `rare`, `big-name`, `occasion`, `tradition`, `unusual` and `major-run`, each with a test the why-line must pass, and an event qualifies under any one of them; the curation prompt holds the tests and real Kansas City examples of each. The model's answer is three-way: `pick` under a reason, `close` (it would understand a friend bringing it up, but no test is clearly met; stored with a one-sentence note, never published, listed in the run report), or `no`, still the default. The reason is data on the event (`pickReason`) and goes to the site, so a reader can see which picks are the scene's (`rare`) and which are everyone's (`big-name`), and disagree with the category and not only the sentence. The split between those two is deliberate: it is the niche-versus-broad line that made the old list narrow. What ADR 0005 got right stands unchanged: no blended score, no ranking, no quota per kind or reason (variety is measured by the report's counts by kind and by reason, and a skew is a prompt bug, not a reason to ration), recurring events never picked, re-judge on change or on a new prompt hash only.

Two other things came with it. A person can now overrule the model from `data/picks.yaml`, a committed file the run reads after curation and never writes: an entry picks an event under a reason with a why-line held to the model's rules, or un-picks one, and stands until it is removed, with the model's own judgment kept beside the event (`modelJudgment`) and restored when it goes; a stale entry (no such active event, one folded into another, a title that no longer matches) is reported, not applied. The file is also the record of where a person disagreed with the model. And curation, the stage whose output is the whole front page, gained the measurement it never had: the report lists every pick and close call in the horizon with its reason or note, says what moved this run and why (the 2026-11-02 prompt edit had flipped ten of sixteen upcoming picks and nothing reported it), and `pnpm compare-curation` judges the eligible events repeatedly under the current prompt, and under a candidate, and scores the flip rate, the pick rate against the prompt's calibration figure (50 to 80 over an eight-week horizon of about 450 eligible events, to be corrected to the truth after two runs), agreement with the stored judgments, and what moves between prompts. A curation prompt edit lands with its comparison report in the same commit. Batches are formed by kind then date, so a batch's neighbours are comparable.

Alternatives considered: widening scarcity alone (giving limited runs their own scarcity shapes) would have kept the selection problem, since the kinds a group gets excited about fail scarcity by construction; a single "would this excite people" question is the blended judgment ADR 0005 rejected and drifts with every edit, as the 11-02 flip showed; quotas by kind would have forced variety without explaining it. The landing stamped every stored pick `rare`, which by construction they all were, so the strict schema held from the first build; the first weekly run after it re-judged every eligible event once through the prompt hash.
