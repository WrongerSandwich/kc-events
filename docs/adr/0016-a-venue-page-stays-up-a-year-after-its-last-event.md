# A venue page stays up a year after its last event

A venue page (#137) was made for every venue among the published events and every venue table row. A venue whose events had all passed, with no row, lost its page at the next build, which the daily deploy makes every night. Search Console on 2026-11-22 showed what that costs (#300): venue pages were the site's second-largest source of impressions, mostly from people searching the venue's own name, and eleven venue URLs that had earned impressions, one of them a click, now answered 404. A venue between seasons, a church with one concert a year or a theatre dark for the summer, would lose whatever place its page had earned each time it went quiet.

So a venue page now stays up for **365 days after the last day of its last published event** (`VENUE_PAGE_DAYS` in `site/src/build/venues.ts`). In those days it shows the same "Nothing listed right now" page a table-row venue with nothing coming up shows, and the venue directory lists it with them. The page stays in the sitemap and gets no `noindex`. Search Console showed only seven pages crawled and not indexed, and venues with one event earned the most venue impressions, so there was no case for dropping thin pages from search. That is to be looked at again after launch.

**What counts.** A record keeps its venue's page if it is `active`, or expired as `past` and verified at least once (`lastVerified`). The job also expires an unverified record as past once its date goes by, and that record was never on the site. A record expired for any other reason (a duplicate, a promotion, an index page, a strike-out, a cancellation, outside the geography) was never a listing the page should keep. The last day is the record's end, else its start. An active record with neither (a recurring one) counts as today. The build reads the records from `data/events.json` and every `data/archive/<year>.json` beside it (`readArchive` in `site/src/build/load-dataset.ts`), because the job archives expired records eight weeks on and a year is longer than that. An archive file that does not parse fails the build, naming the file.

**What does not.** A record in an `outsidePlaces` place (ADR 0015) keeps nothing; the Lawrence venues' pages are meant to go. Nor does a venue that names no one place: a street address or generic words, which the loader already warns about when an active event has one. Nobody searches for "1120 Central Avenue" as a venue, and keeping those pages would only add thin ones.

**Where a kept page places the venue.** Its name and neighborhood come from its records within the year, the most recent first: the spelling and neighborhood most of them give, the most recent breaking a tie, the same way a page with events takes them from its events.

**Renames are not followed.** When a venue's display name changes (an alias added, a page that starts spelling it another way), its slug changes with it, and the old slug stays up as a quiet page under this rule until its year runs out. There is no redirect from it; that would need a rename table no one keeps (#300 left it out, and #303 is about past events' URLs, not venues). Old spellings in the dataset ("The Midland Theatre - MO" beside the current name) each keep a quiet page for the year in the same way.

On the 11-16 run's data, with no archive yet, the rule takes the venue pages from 102 to 220. 120 of them list nothing, two of those by a table row as before.
